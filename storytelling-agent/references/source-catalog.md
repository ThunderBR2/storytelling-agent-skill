### S01 — Robert McKee, *Story: Substance, Structure, Style, and the Principles of Screenwriting*

- File: `Livro/Robert McKee - Story - OCR completo.txt`
- Type: OCR of full book.
- Medium focus: screenwriting / long-form dramatic structure.
- Key contributions:
  - Principles over rules; forms over formulas.
  - Archetype vs stereotype.
  - Story design terminology: beat, scene, sequence, act, story.
  - Values and value change.
  - Inciting incident, progressive complications, crisis, climax, resolution.
  - Scene design and scene analysis.
  - Gap between expectation and result.
  - Principle of antagonism.
  - Exposition as ammunition, not information dump.
  - Character revealed by choices under pressure.
  - Controlling idea/theme.
- Useful when:
  - Diagnosing structure, scene function, causality, conflict, stakes, climax, and exposition.
- Limitations:
  - Strongly oriented toward feature-length dramatic screenwriting. Do not apply feature logic automatically to 15–60s social videos or ads.

### S02 — Lofi Cinema Masterclass, “Work Your Ideas”

Files:

- `Masterclass/Masterclass Lofi Cinema - 01 - Intro.txt`
- `Masterclass/Masterclass Lofi Cinema - 02 - Chapter 1 - Good movies vs Bad movies.txt`
- `Masterclass/Masterclass Lofi Cinema - 03 - Chapter 2 - Learning to deconstruct.txt`
- `Masterclass/Masterclass Lofi Cinema - 04 - Chapter 3 - Working with your core.txt`
- `Masterclass/Masterclass Lofi Cinema - 05 - Chapter 4 - What makes a good core.txt`
- `Masterclass/Masterclass Lofi Cinema - 06 - Chapter 5 - What makes a story original.txt`
- `Masterclass/Masterclass Lofi Cinema - 07 - Chapter 6 - Embracing clichés.txt`
- `Masterclass/Masterclass Lofi Cinema - 08 - P.S..txt`

- Type: Patreon masterclass transcript.
- Medium focus: idea development, originality, premise/core relationship, deconstruction.
- Key contributions:
  - Two common starting paths: clever premise outward-in; personal experience inward-out.
  - Premise = facade / surface promise; core = what the story is actually about underneath.
  - Representation = the chosen fictional/audiovisual expression of the core.
  - A story feels generic when a clever premise carries a safe or pasted-on emotional engine.
  - A personal experience can become cliché if translated through familiar metaphors.
  - Good core should grow organically from the premise.
  - Originality comes mostly from core and its relationship to premise, not surface novelty.
  - Cliché vs convention; stereotype vs archetype.
  - Simple or familiar premises can become meaningful through transformation.
- Examples named in the material:
  - *Gravity*, *In Bruges*, *Inside Out*, *Passengers*, *In Time*, *The Last of Us*, *Pluribus*, *Little Miss Sunshine*, and other cases used to discuss premise/core/representation.
- Useful when:
  - Developing raw ideas, finding the real story beneath a premise, avoiding clever-but-empty concepts, reframing clichés.
- Limitations:
  - Less a universal structure system than an idea-development methodology. Pair with diagnostics for execution problems.

### S04 — Dan Erickson, “On Writing Severance”

- File: `Extra/02 - On Writing Severance - A Conversation with Dan Erickson.txt`
- Type: interview transcript.
- Medium focus: TV series, mystery/sci-fi premise, workplace dystopia, character comedy.
- Key contributions:
  - Premise can emerge from a painful personal wish or emotional contradiction.
  - High-concept world works when it dramatizes an ordinary human feeling.
  - Tone can combine dark premise with character-based comedy.
  - Mystery should be grounded in character and institutional logic.
- Useful when:
  - Building high-concept series premises, mystery engines, and tonal contrast.
- Limitations:
  - TV/series-specific lessons do not automatically solve short-form or feature structure.

- Medium focus: fiction, narrative shapes, humanism, clarity.
- Key contributions:

### S06 — George R. R. Martin, “Write What You Know”

## Cross-source Themes

- **Principles not formulas:** McKee explicitly frames story as principles over rules; the user's instruction reinforces this.
- **Surface vs depth:** Lofi Cinema premise/core; Martin emotional truth; Docter/Pixar emotional grounding; Erickson high-concept workplace as emotional metaphor.
- **Causality and consequence:** McKee's structure/scene logic; user instruction emphasizes because/therefore over and-then.
- **Character pressure:** McKee choices under pressure; Severance's trapped characters; Martin's emotional truth.
- **Originality as transformation:** Lofi Cinema cliche/convention; Schrader personal/formal metaphor; Vonnegut simple shapes handled with clarity.
- **Visual/audiovisual embodiment:** McKee screenwriting, Schrader cinema form, Docter animation, user's visual priority.

## Known Tensions / Contradictions to Preserve

- **Structure vs anti-formula:** McKee offers detailed structural concepts while explicitly warning against formulas. Apply concepts diagnostically, not as templates.
- **Clever premise vs emotional truth:** Lofi warns clever premises can be hollow; Erickson and Pixar examples show high concepts can work when grounded emotionally.
- **Cliché vs convention:** Lofi treats familiar premises as usable if the core transforms them. Do not reject familiar ideas automatically.
- **Diagram/shape vs living story:** Vonnegut's shapes are useful emotional maps but should not replace causal, character-based analysis.
- **Authorial obsession vs audience clarity:** Schrader's authorial/formal emphasis can conflict with broad-audience clarity; choose based on project intent.
