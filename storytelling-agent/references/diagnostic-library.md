# Diagnostic Library — Storytelling Agent

Use this as the Story Doctor layer: `SYMPTOM → POSSIBLE CAUSES → TESTS → SOLUTIONS`. Diagnose before rewriting.

## D01 — “Tenho uma ideia, mas ainda não existe história”

### Possible Causes

- Premise exists but no core.
- Surface novelty creates false sense of progress.
- No protagonist or desire.
- No dramatic question.
- No antagonistic force.
- No stakes.
- Personal feeling exists but representation is generic.

### Tests

- What does the audience think the story is about?
- What is it really about underneath?
- Who is changed or revealed by this premise?
- What decision becomes unavoidable?
- Why this premise for this core?

### Solutions

- Generate 3–5 possible cores under the premise.
- Choose a protagonist whose desire collides with the core.
- Define dramatic question and final image.
- Find representation that makes the emotional truth visible.
- Build a causal spine: because → therefore → but → therefore.

## D02 — “Meu segundo ato está chato”

### Possible Causes

- Objective established but not evolving.
- Stakes remain static.
- Protagonist reacts instead of acts.
- Scenes repeat same conflict.
- No new information or reversals.
- Antagonistic force is weak.
- Midpoint does not reorient story.
- Causality is “and then” rather than “therefore.”

### Tests

- Does each sequence change the plan, stakes, information, or relationship?
- Does the protagonist make increasingly costly choices?
- What becomes harder after each attempt?
- If scenes are rearranged, does anything break? If not, causality is weak.
- Is there a midpoint reversal or discovery?

### Solutions

- Add progressive complications.
- Force a new strategy from the protagonist.
- Raise consequence in a specific domain.
- Reveal information that changes interpretation.
- Create a reversal: success causes worse problem, or failure reveals opportunity.
- Compress repeated scenes into one stronger scene.

## D03 — “Preciso contar essa história em 60 segundos”

### Possible Causes

- Feature/short logic being applied to micro-format.
- Too many characters or beats.
- Exposition competing with action.
- No single dramatic question.
- Payoff depends on context not available in time.

### Tests

- Can the story be understood as hook → conflict/turn → payoff?
- What is the one emotional movement?
- What can be removed without breaking the core?
- What can be shown visually instead of explained?
- What final image carries the payoff?

### Solutions

- Keep one protagonist, one desire, one obstacle, one turn.
- Replace backstory with a visible clue.
- Merge setup with action.
- Use visual contrast and object-based payoff.
- End on final image rather than explanation.

## D04 — “Quero um final mais impactante”

### Possible Causes

- Dramatic question unclear.
- Climax does not force a decision.
- Setup/payoff missing.
- Stakes are too low or unchanged.
- Ending resolves plot but not emotion/theme.
- Transformation is stated but not proven.
- Twist surprises but does not reframe.

### Tests

- What question did the beginning ask?
- What choice does the protagonist make at maximum pressure?
- What earlier setup is paid off?
- What value changes in the climax?
- What final image proves the change?
- Would the ending feel inevitable in hindsight?

### Solutions

- Clarify dramatic question earlier.
- Make climax a choice, not just an event.
- Add/strengthen setup that pays off in the ending.
- Increase cost of final decision.
- Convert verbal resolution into final action/image.
- Reframe ending as moral choice, sacrifice, reversal, recognition, or consequence.

## D05 — “Crie três caminhos para essa história”

### Possible Causes

- User needs divergent development, not polish.
- Premise supports multiple cores.

### Tests

- Are options using different mechanisms or just cosmetic variations?
- Does each path imply a different protagonist, conflict, emotional movement, and ending?

### Solutions

Offer genuinely distinct strategies, e.g.:

- Emotional: transformation/relation.
- Suspense: information asymmetry and reveal.
- Comic: expectation reversal and escalation.
- Moral: impossible choice.
- Existential: reinterpretation of meaning.
- Visual: metaphor/action without dialogue.

For each option include mechanism, emotional effect, risk, and best format.

## D06 — “Essa história está boa?”

### Possible Causes

- User wants critique but may be asking generally.
- Need evaluate both promise and execution.

### Tests

- Is the premise clear?
- Does the story have a core beneath the premise?
- Is the protagonist active?
- Are stakes specific?
- Is conflict escalating?
- Does every major beat cause the next?
- Is the ending earned?
- Is there cliché without transformation?

### Solutions

- Give concise Story Score.
- Name top 2 strengths and top 3 weaknesses.
- Identify root problem.
- Recommend efficient next revision, not total rewrite unless necessary.

## D07 — “Quero contar tudo sem diálogo”

### Possible Causes

- Story depends on abstract information.
- Character psychology is stated rather than dramatized.
- World rules are explained verbally.
- Theme is not embodied in action.

### Tests

- What would the audience understand with sound muted?
- Which emotions are visible in behavior?
- Which objects/environment can carry information?
- What changes visually from first image to final image?
- Can relationship dynamics be shown through distance, touch, blocking, gaze, or rhythm?

### Solutions

- Convert exposition into action, object, gesture, environment, contrast.
- Use setup/payoff through props.
- Use blocking to show power/relationship.
- Use sound/silence/music for inner state.
- Use final image as thematic resolution.

## D08 — “De onde veio essa recomendação?”

### Possible Causes

- User wants provenance.
- Need distinguish source from synthesis/inference.

### Tests

- Is the recommendation directly traceable to source catalog?
- Is it a normalized principle across sources?
- Is it my application to the user’s material?

### Solutions

Respond in three layers:

1. SOURCE KNOWLEDGE — explicit source if verified.
2. GENERAL STORYTELLING PRINCIPLE — normalized idea.
3. AGENT INFERENCE — why it applies here.

Do not invent a citation.

## D09 — Low stakes

### Possible Causes

- Consequences are vague.
- Protagonist can walk away.
- Failure does not affect relationships, identity, morality, or future.
- Stakes do not escalate.
- Audience does not understand why it matters to this character.

### Tests

- What happens if the protagonist quits?
- Who is harmed or changed by failure?
- What becomes irreversible?
- What personal value is threatened?
- Are stakes visible, not just stated?

### Solutions

- Make consequence specific and irreversible.
- Tie external goal to internal wound/belief.
- Add relational or moral cost.
- Escalate after each attempt.
- Show stakes through action/object/relationship.

## D10 — Passive protagonist

### Possible Causes

- Goal belongs to another character.
- Protagonist only reacts.
- Plot events solve problems for them.
- Fear/flaw has no pressure.
- No costly choices.

### Tests

- What decision changes the story?
- Could the same plot happen if protagonist were removed?
- Does the protagonist cause the climax?
- What strategy do they try, fail, and change?

### Solutions

- Give protagonist a clear want.
- Force choices with cost.
- Make attempts cause complications.
- Ensure climax results from protagonist action/decision.
- Transfer key agency from mentor/world/coincidence to protagonist.

## D11 — Cliché / predictable idea

### Possible Causes

- Familiar premise with familiar core.
- Expected metaphor.
- Stock character roles.
- First obvious solution used.
- Genre convention played without transformation.

### Tests

- What is the first obvious version?
- What is the second predictable version?
- What is the third version that remains clear but less expected?
- Is the cliché in premise or in core?
- Can the familiar setup become convention by deepening execution?

### Solutions

- Shift the core, not just surface details.
- Change protagonist relationship to premise.
- Add contradiction or moral ambiguity.
- Use a more specific emotional truth.
- Reframe convention through fresh final image/payoff.

## D12 — Exposition problem

### Possible Causes

- Dialogue explains backstory/world/theme.
- Information arrives before audience wants it.
- No pressure in information delivery.
- Visual evidence missing.

### Tests

- Does the audience need this now?
- Can the information be discovered through conflict?
- Can it be shown by object, behavior, setting, or consequence?
- What happens if this line is cut?

### Solutions

- Delay until audience asks the question.
- Attach information to obstacle or choice.
- Replace explanation with visible clue.
- Split exposition into setup/payoff.
- Let characters lie, avoid, or weaponize information.

## D13 — Weak twist

### Possible Causes

- Surprise is arbitrary.
- No setup.
- Twist changes facts but not meaning.
- No consequence for character/stakes.
- Audience could not retrospectively re-read clues.

### Tests

- What changes after the twist?
- What earlier details gain new meaning?
- Who must make a new decision because of it?
- Does it reveal character/theme or only plot?

### Solutions

- Plant fair but ambiguous clues.
- Make reveal alter objective/stakes/relationship.
- Tie twist to protagonist flaw or desire.
- Add consequence immediately after reveal.
- Consider dramatic irony instead of hidden twist.

## D14 — Weak visual potential

### Possible Causes

- Story depends on talk, ideas, or abstract context.
- Stakes are invisible.
- Character change has no external marker.
- Setting is unused.

### Tests

- What are the three strongest images?
- What object can transform meaning?
- How does the environment pressure the character?
- What visual contrast marks beginning vs ending?

### Solutions

- Externalize internal conflict through task/action.
- Create symbolic but specific object.
- Use blocking and distance as relationship language.
- Build a final image that reverses or completes the first image.

## D15 — Theme feels forced

### Possible Causes

- Theme stated in dialogue.
- Plot does not test values.
- Character choices do not embody the question.
- Ending asserts meaning not earned by action.

### Tests

- What values are in conflict?
- What choice tests the theme?
- What does the ending prove through consequence?
- Could the theme be removed without changing plot? If yes, it is not integrated.

### Solutions

- Turn theme into dilemma.
- Give opposing characters valid values.
- Make climax choose between competing goods/bads.
- Replace thematic speech with irreversible action.
